## LEX10.1 Purpose
Large exposure rules limit the maximum loss a bank could face if a counterparty fails suddenly, so that the loss does not threaten the bank's solvency.

## LEX10.4 Level of application
The large exposures framework applies at every tier within a banking group, matching the scope of the risk-based capital requirements.

## LEX10.6 Consolidated measurement
At consolidated level a bank compares its aggregate group exposures to third parties with the group's Tier 1 capital.

## LEX10.7 Exempt counterparties
Only sovereigns are exempt from the counterparty scope of the framework. Other exemptions and special treatments are set out in LEX30.31 to LEX30.59.

## LEX10.8 Definition of a large exposure
A large exposure is the sum of exposure values to a counterparty or a group of connected counterparties that equals or exceeds 10% of the bank's Tier 1 capital.

## LEX10.9 Connected counterparties
A group of connected counterparties is treated as a single counterparty for both the limit and reporting purposes.

## LEX10.12 Control presumption
Two or more persons are connected through a control relationship or economic interdependence. Owning more than 50% of voting rights automatically satisfies the control criterion.

## LEX10.18 Interdependence review threshold
Banks must identify possible economic interdependencies where the total exposure to one counterparty exceeds 5% of Tier 1 capital.

## LEX20.1 Large exposure limit
The sum of a bank's exposure values to a single counterparty or a group of connected counterparties must not be higher than 25% of the bank's Tier 1 capital at all times. The limit is 15% for the exposures of a global systemically important bank (G-SIB) to another G-SIB.

## LEX20.3 Breaches
Breaches of the large exposure limit should be rare. A breach must be reported to the supervisor immediately and corrected quickly.

## LEX20.4 Regulatory reporting
Banks must report exposure values before and after credit risk mitigation to the supervisor. Reporting covers all exposures at or above 10% of Tier 1 capital, other exposures at or above 10% measured without credit risk mitigation, all exempted exposures at or above 10%, and the bank's largest 20 exposures to in-scope counterparties regardless of size.

## LEX30.4 Measuring derivative exposures
For instruments that carry counterparty credit risk, other than securities financing transactions, the exposure value is the exposure at default under the standardised approach for counterparty credit risk (SA-CCR).

## LEX30.15 Banking and trading book aggregation
Exposures in the banking book and the trading book both count toward a single counterparty total. Netting across the two books is not permitted.

## LEX30.31 Sovereign exemption
Exposures to sovereigns, their central banks, and public sector entities treated as sovereigns are exempt, but they must still be reported if they meet the large exposure definition.

## LEX30.35 Intraday interbank exposures
Intraday interbank exposures are not subject to the framework, for either reporting or the limit.

## LEX-NOTE Intragroup position (digest note, not a Basel paragraph)
LEX30 does not set out a separate treatment for intragroup exposures. Group relationships are considered through the general connected-counterparty rules in LEX10, so a bank must check the national rule that applies to it.
